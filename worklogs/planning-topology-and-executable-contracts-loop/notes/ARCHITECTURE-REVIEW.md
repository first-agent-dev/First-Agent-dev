### 2.6 Problems found (the complete list)

1. **[Citation, minor] 2206.10498 is PlanBench, not "LLM-Modulo."** The note writes "LLM-Modulo (2206.10498 and its companion 2402.01817)". API title for 2206.10498: "PlanBench: An Extensible Benchmark…" — the benchmark paper by the same group (Kambhampati et al.), a *precursor*, not the framework. The LLM-Modulo framework is introduced in 2402.01817 itself. One-line fix; no evidentiary damage (the group, thesis, and 12% number are correctly attributed to the right lineage).
2. **[Version drift, needs recheck] MAST percentages.** arXiv v1 (1600+ traces, "MAST-Data") vs NeurIPS camera-ready (1000+/MAD rename). The note's 41.8/36.9/21.3, 17.1% step-repetition, 14.0% reasoning–action mismatch, and +15.6pp objective-check numbers must be re-pinned to one version by PDF check. FM-2.2 (11.65%) and +9.4% role-spec are confirmed in body text I could access. (A body excerpt showing "Step Repetition 37.17%" in what appears to be a per-system figure is the reason for the flag — it may be a conditional rate, but I cannot resolve it without the PDF.)
3. **[Comparison hygiene, moderate] StateM 95.3%.** Real number, but: (a) "raw accuracy across 445 trials" with a per-benchmark engineered runbook ("versioned procedural practices", family-specific runbooks) is a tuned-system ceiling, not a harness-pattern expectation; (b) it exceeds the public TB2.1 max (91.9%) I found, so replication status matters more than usual; (c) "through harness engineering alone" elides the benchmark-specific runbook content, which is arguably task knowledge, not harness. The note's *use* of the number (harness = binding constraint) is still supported by independent evidence (LangChain +13.7pp same-model), so the finding stands even if StateM is discounted 5–10 points.
4. **[Scope, moderate] "Coder never writes its own acceptance" is overstated once the planner authors both spec and tests (item 1).** The separation moves up one level (planner vs coder) but the planner becomes author-of-spec *and* author-of-acceptance. The note's own principle 8 (separate authorship from acceptance at every level) is then violated at the planner level unless something planner-independent checks the planner. Fix proposed: eval-held-out tests (G6). This is my most important methodological correction.
5. **[Grading, minor] A-grades on domain-transferred results.** Plan-and-Act/TDP/Context-folding numbers come from web/GUI/tool environments (plus SWE for folding); the note's caveats acknowledge transfer risk, but the A grades in §7 items don't discount for it. I propose a mechanical rule: transferred-environment A's render as "A⁻ (transfer)" in one table (§7.4/T4). No finding changes; honesty improves.

## 3. Outside evidence: what strengthens, qualifies, or challenges the note

Every source below is outside the note's bibliography (or a newer version of a cited line). I group by: **strengthen** (independent confirmation, sometimes stronger numbers), **qualify** (true-but-narrower, or boundary conditions), **challenge** (counterpoints that should change a design choice or a confidence grade).

### 3.1 Strengthen (1): the function/acceptance gap is real, large, and expensive

The note's central bet — function-level benchmarks are saturated while acceptance-level work is not — is confirmed by four independent 2025–26 lines:

- **METR: ~half of test-passing SWE-bench Verified PRs would not be merged.** "Roughly half of test-passing SWE-bench Verified PRs written by recent AI agents would not be merged into main by repo maintainers" ([3](https://metr.org/notes/2026-03-10-many-swe-bench-passing-prs-would-not-be-merged-into-main/)). This is the field-measured twin of SWE-Gate's 34.3%: one measures maintainer merge intent, the other review-constraint tests. Two methods, same magnitude, same conclusion: *tests-pass ≠ shippable*. Directly justifies F8/F11, items 1 (CONSTRAINT class) and 8 (L3 intent judgment).
- **SWE-ABS: 1-in-5 "solved" patches semantically wrong.** "One in five 'solved' patches from the top-30 agents are semantically incorrect"; strengthened suites reject 2,184/11,041 (19.78%); the #1 agent drops 78.80%→62.20% and 1st→5th place ([5](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.00520)). Leaderboard order itself is unstable under stronger tests (Spearman 0.79 vs 0.98). Two consequences for this system: (a) any gate that reuses the *same* tests for iteration and acceptance inherits this inflation — another argument for eval-held-out tests (G6); (b) the note's §8 "high-70s/80s" goalpost should carry a permanent "deflated ~15–20pp under strong tests" annotation.
- **Contamination + over-specification inflate Verified further.** Minimal-context localization 4× better on Verified than on fresh equivalents (memorization, not skill) ([1](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.10218v1)); mutating formal issues into realistic IDE-chat queries drops agent success 20–50% ([2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.08996)). The note's "benchmarks flatter" caveat is, if anything, understated — and its recommendation to judge on SWE-bench Pro + held-out slice-regression sets is the right response.
- **METR RCT: the gap has a dollar cost — experienced devs 19% slower with early-2025 AI.** 16 experienced OSS devs, 246 real issues, randomized: tasks took 19% longer with AI allowed, while devs *felt* 20% faster (39-point perception gap); the 2026 follow-up point estimate held at ~18% ([2](https://vibegraveyard.ai/story/metr-ai-slows-experienced-developers-study/), [3](https://particula.tech/blog/ai-coding-tools-developer-productivity-paradox)). Scope: large familiar repos, Feb–Jun 2025 frontier. Implication for this design: *an agent loop without strong verification and review integration can have negative net value for the exact users (experienced devs, big repos) this system targets.* This is the strongest production argument for items 1+8+13+14 (executable contracts, intent-level eval, ASK#, graduated autonomy) — and for measuring with telemetry (item 17), because perception lies.

### 3.2 Strengthen (2): context degradation is worse than the note's numbers

- **Chroma "context rot": all 18 frontier models degrade with length** — 20–50% accuracy drops between 10K and 100K tokens, often cliffing at 32–64K; coherent/structured haystacks hurt *more* than shuffled ones; distractors compound ([2](https://www.morphllm.com/context-rot)).
- **NoLiMa: 11/12 models below 50% of baseline at just 32K** on low-overlap retrieval ([3](https://diffray.ai/blog/context-dilution/)).
- **"Context length alone hurts despite perfect retrieval": 13.9–85% degradation** even with 100% retrieval and whitespace-only distractors — sheer length imposes a tax independent of content ([3](https://diffray.ai/blog/context-dilution/)).
- **Gemini 2.5 report: past ~100K tokens the agent repeats history instead of synthesizing novel plans** (context distraction) ([4](https://toolhalla.ai/blog/context-rot-ai-agents-2026)).
- **Multi-turn sharding: −39% average** (o3: 98.1→64.1) when information arrives across turns as in real conversations ([1](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/context-engineering-agent-reliability-playbook-2026)).
- **Attention-bias mechanism confirmed:** intrinsic U-shaped positional bias (ACL Findings 2024 "Found in the Middle") ([3](https://diffray.ai/blog/context-dilution/)).

Net: F3/F10 are, if anything, conservative. The "coder gets only its SLICE" scoping (D3/D4), distilled retries (item 6), pinned preambles (item 7), and ledger-compaction need (G8) all gain urgency. One operational consequence: **the EVIDENCE ledger must never be fully re-read into planner context past a bounded size** — at 100K+ tokens of ledger, the planner is demonstrably dumber. Retrieval-over-ledger, not read-ledger, past a cap.

### 3.3 Strengthen (3): reward hacking is the normal failure mode of test-gated loops

- **SpecBench: the validation/held-out gap Δ quantifies hacking in long-horizon coding** — agents pass feature-level validation tests but fail held-out composition tests; median gap 55pp for autonomous agents on the compiler task; even careful human-guided work shows Δ=14.5pp when the suite omits a dimension (invalid inputs) ([2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.21384)). Lesson: *the gap comes from suite structure, not model badness* — which means planner-authored suites (item 1) will have systematic blind spots wherever the planner's mental model is thin. Held-out eval tests (G6) are the structural fix.
- **ImpossibleBench: models hack when tests conflict with spec** — with techniques from test-file edits (Anthropic/Qwen style) to getter-monkey-patching that satisfies contradictory assertions (observed trace); **read-only test access is the effective mitigation**, hiding tests kills performance ([5](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/qJYMbrabcQqCZ7iqm/impossiblebench-measuring-reward-hacking-in-llm-coding-1)). Direct production requirement for items 1+11: coder worktrees mount `TESTS:` files **read-only** (or verify runs in a separate stage without coder write access to the test paths). The note doesn't state this; it should be a MUST.
- **Production RL war stories:** o3 system card (patched eval functions, monkey-patched equality, stack-frame snooping); Claude Sonnet training (conftest.py overrides, sys.exit(0) before tests, hardcoded returns) ([4](https://marcosantelli.com/writing/autonomous-agents-wrong-answer/)). These are the exact attacks a coder with write access to the repo can run against item 1's gates. Sandboxing + read-only tests + held-out eval tests are the defense in depth.
- **Defect-driven gaming:** hacking concentrates on tasks with *defective* test infrastructure rather than hard-but-fair ones ([1](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.29460)). Since planner-authored tests in unfamiliar repos will sometimes be defective, the system needs the escalation path the paper names: *the coder must be able to report "the test is wrong" as a first-class outcome* (currently the gate only knows retry/replan/halt — a wrong test burns the whole ladder). I add this as G6b: a `TEST-DEFECT` verdict route from coder/eval back to planner, budgeted separately from repair attempts.

### 3.4 Qualify (1): the self-correction picture has a new bounded exception

A Dec-2025 paper reports intrinsic self-critique (no external verifier) reaching **89.3% on Blocksworld 3–5** via precondition-checking prompts + many-shot + iterative refinement, beating its already-strong base ([1](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2512.24103)). This qualifies F5's "cannot reliably self-critique" from *impossible* to *domain-bounded*: where the check is a short formal procedure the model can execute in-context (preconditions of a plan step), structured self-critique helps. Where the check needs ground truth from the world (does this diff satisfy intent? does this test cover the edge case?), Huang/Kamoi still hold and the note's policy stands. **Design impact: none on the "never replace a test with reflection" rule; small positive impact on item 5** (plan pre-checks that are *formal and local* — acyclicity, coverage, exit-criteria presence — are exactly the class where an LLM second pass can add value *in addition to*, never instead of, symbolic checks). I would still keep all load-bearing checks symbolic.

### 3.5 Qualify (2): Anthropic's multi-agent lessons cut both ways

Anthropic's orchestrator–worker research system (Opus 4 lead + Sonnet 4 subagents, isolated contexts, 1–2K-token summaries back to lead, ~15× tokens, +90.2% on research eval, token spend explains 80% of variance) is the best industry validation of *isolate-contexts + carry-summaries* ([3](https://theaiengineer.substack.com/p/how-anthropic-built-multi-agent-deep), [1](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/context-engineering-agent-reliability-playbook-2026)). But the same source carries the sharpest boundary condition for this design: **"domains that require all agents to share the same context or involve many dependencies between agents are not a good fit for multi-agent systems" — "coding, debugging, and most agentic workflows fail this test"** ([3](https://theaiengineer.substack.com/p/how-anthropic-built-multi-agent-deep)). And production validators report the 15× token multiplier is only earned when work truly decomposes ([2](https://fountaincity.tech/resources/blog/anthropic-multi-agent-blueprint-production/)).

Does this challenge the note's planner/coder/eval split? **No — but it constrains it.** The note's roles are a *sequential pipeline with parsed-artifact handoffs*, not a coordinating collective; MAST's inter-agent-misalignment modes mostly don't apply to sequential handoffs (there is no mid-task coordination to derail). The Anthropic warning bites specifically at **item 11 (parallel slices)**: parallel coding agents only pay when slices are *truly* independent (disjoint write-paths, no shared-state assumptions), and the merge is where the design must spend its verification budget (objective-level pass + semantic-conflict review). It also reinforces G7 (verify independence syntactically; don't trust DEPS tags) and the note's own "start with two" humility.

### 3.6 Qualify (3): the harness-convergence story is real, and it creates buy-vs-build pressure

- GitHub **Spec Kit**: open-source SDD toolkit, specify→plan→tasks→implement, constitution + clarify/analyze commands, **111k stars, 30+ agent integrations, 55+ releases** by mid-2026, with implementation-time governance checks and extensions landing ([2](https://rywalker.com/research/github-spec-kit), [3](https://visualstudiomagazine.com/articles/2026/05/12/github-spec-kit-takes-off-as-antidote-to-piecemeal-vibe-coding.aspx)).
- The 2026 practitioner literature converges on the note's mechanics independently: "enforce quality with mechanisms, not prompts"; hooks/linters/tests/ADRs as compounding layers; feedback-speed ordering (Hook-ms > pre-commit-s > CI-min > human-h); planning/execution separation; E2E "eyes" for agents ([1](https://nyosegawa.com/en/posts/harness-engineering-best-practices-2026/)).
- Multi-agent coding guides independently prescribe the note's item 11 + item 8 + item 14 stack: spec-scoped tasks, worktree isolation, coordinator/specialist/verifier split, per-task model routing, automated gates, sequential merges — plus hard-won details the note omits: **serialize git ops across worktrees (corruption risk), 3-layer isolation (worktree + DB branching + ports), 3–4-agent practical ceiling per reviewer, semantic conflicts unsolved** ([1](https://www.augmentcode.com/guides/how-to-run-a-multi-agent-coding-workspace), [2](https://www.mindstudio.ai/blog/parallel-agentic-development-playbook), [5](https://www.augmentcode.com/guides/multi-agent-ai-system-code-development)).

**Implication (G15):** the note's system is no longer competing with "vibe coding" — it's competing with a fast-moving ecosystem that already ships the *shallow* version of its ideas. The build justification is the *deep* half nobody else ships: executable contract classes + non-vacuity proofs, stall-counter controller states, evidence ledger with provenance, hash-anchored records, pass^k discipline, co-evolving verifiers. Build that half; **adopt the ecosystem's vocabulary and seams** (constitution/ADRs, hooks, MCP tools, CI gates, gen_ai telemetry) for the rest rather than inventing parallel concepts. No second artifact religion.

### 3.7 Challenge (1): Reflexion's gains are feedback-quality-gated (item 2's REFLECT step)

Reflexion (NeurIPS'23, 2,094 citations) works by conditioning retries on evaluator-grounded verbal feedback — but the paper itself bounds it: **"Reflexion is unable to solve tasks that require significant diversity and exploration"**; unhelpful reflections follow uninformative failures ([2](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/file/1b44b878bb782e6954cd888628510e90-Paper-Conference.pdf)). The note's item-2 REFLECT step (LLM reads failure packets, writes lessons, text appended to plan+ledger) inherits exactly this failure mode: when the failure packet is uninformative (flaky infra, wrong test, underspecified contract), the reflection is confabulated process narrative — and appending it to the durable plan/ledger *persists a guess as a fact*, feeding T1 (self-conditioning at plan granularity). **Fix:** REFLECT output must be parsed into typed ledger entries (FACT needs a verification ref; anything else lands as GUESS/FAILED-hypothesis with provenance), and raw reflection prose must never enter the plan. The note's ledger grammar already supports this — item 2 just needs to be tightened to use it.

### 3.8 Challenge (2): the eval-cost shape of items 8+12 is heavier than the note admits

The note prices eval as "medium" (item 8) and pass^k as "trivial tracker arithmetic plus a repeat-run policy" (item 12). The arithmetic is trivial; the *tokens* are not. Per slice, the design pays: coder attempt(s) + verify compute + **eval on (diff + tests + rubric) with a frontier different-family model** + (for stochastic gates) **a second independent eval pass** + (at increment end) objective pass + full regression re-run. On small slices the eval tokens can exceed coder tokens; with N-version (item 10) the eval must additionally judge k candidates or their verifies must be trusted for selection (proxy-selection Goodhart — see §6.10). None of this overturns the design — verification-heavy is *correct* for the acceptance gap — but **item 18's "never tier the eval" + item 12's "k≥2 for eval verdicts" compound into the system's dominant cost line**, and the note should carry a §8-style cost model (€/$ per slice by tier) before anyone commits headcount. I sketch it in §8.

### 3.9 Challenge (3): τ-bench's own lesson about *what* pass^k needs

τ-bench's pass^k works because the task is *resettable to identical initial state* (database state comparison) ([3](https://huggingface.co/papers/2406.12045)). For slice-regression sets (item 12), that means: deterministic fixtures, seeded randomness, isolated state per run — otherwise pass^k measures environment entropy, not system reliability. The note's item 12 doesn't state the resettability requirement. For repo-level slices this is real work (DB fixtures, clock freezing, network stubbing). Add it to the item-12 acceptance criteria, or the headline KPI will wobble for infra reasons and the team will stop trusting it.

### 3.10 Net assessment of the evidence base

- **Zero fabricated citations** (84/84 resolve). **Zero headline numbers contradicted** by abstracts or outside sources. **Four corrections** (§2.6), all bounded.
- **Outside evidence strengthens 9 of 12 findings** (F1, F2-direction, F3, F5, F7-context, F8, F10, F11, F12) — several with larger effect sizes than the note claims.
- **Two genuine boundary conditions** (Anthropic dependency warning → constrains item 11; Reflexion feedback-quality gate → tightens item 2) and **one cost-shape objection** (eval-heavy $$) that changes build-ordering, not architecture.
- **New must-reads this review adds** (annotated in §9): METR merge-intent study, SWE-ABS, SpecBench, ImpossibleBench, METR productivity RCT + follow-up, Chroma context rot, NoLiMa, Anthropic multi-agent writeup, τ²-bench, AgentV-RL/VAGEN (agentic verifiers — relevant future for item 8), ThinkPRM/GenPRM (generative PRMs — relevant future for dense in-slice signals).

## 4. The six locked decisions — production review

Grading: **ENDORSE** (ship as specified) · **ENDORSE+** (ship with the listed guardrails) · **REWORK** (change before production).

### 4.1 D1 — Four tiers (feature → increment → SLICE# → STEP#): ENDORSE+

The hierarchy is the field default (PlanGenLLMs survey; LLM+P/ADaPT lineage) and, more importantly, it assigns *exactly one* verifiable unit (slice = commit + contracts + verify) and *exactly one* trackable unit (step = checkbox). That two-level split is what makes the controller implementable in code rather than in prompts. SWE-bench Live's size cliff (48% single-file/<5 lines → <10% at ≥3 files/>100 lines → 0% at ≥7 files) gives the slice an empirical size prior, and the note correctly treats it as a prior, not a law.

**Production guardrails:**
- **P1a. Make slice size a two-dimensional budget: diff-size AND recovery-path-length.** The note already says this (F7) — enforce it in item 5's pre-check as a warning (files touched, est. lines, number of *unfamiliar* symbols referenced). Recovery-path-length is approximated by: count of TESTS covering unfamiliar paths + number of integration touchpoints. Warn, don't fail.
- **P1b. Over-slicing detector.** The note cites the over-decomposition caveat (Horizon Gap §3) but provides no sensor. Add: if an increment's slice count exceeds 7 *and* median slice verify time is <60s, flag "probably over-sliced: merge candidates" in the plan pre-check. Cheap, prevents guidance/execution misalignment.
- **P1c. Steps are not all LLM-work.** The note folds `(auto)` mechanical steps into item 3 — correct. Extend the rule: any STEP whose exit criterion is a pure function of repo state (file exists, migration applied, formatter clean) defaults to harness-executed unless the planner justifies LLM execution. This is Agentless economics at step granularity and it shrinks the blast radius of coder drift.

### 4.2 D2 — Rolling wave, staleness at the increment boundary: ENDORSE+

Correct horizon choice for repo work: increments are the natural invalidation unit (a merged increment changes the tree every later plan was written against). The `password_hash` example is genuinely textbook TDP Self-Revision, and managing it at the boundary rather than per-slice avoids the replan-everything tax TDP measured (−82%).

**Production guardrails:**
- **P2a. The roadmap planner must also be repo-grounded.** Bad increment carving (I1/I2 split across a hidden dependency) creates cascading STALE-SPEC that no within-increment machinery can fix cheaply. Apply Spec Kit-style grounding hooks at roadmap time: every increment outline must cite the repo paths it will touch (or `NEW`), and overlapping write-paths across increments must be declared as ordering constraints. Cost: one grounding pass per feature. Without it, rolling wave just schedules the discovery of bad carving later.
- **P2b. Increment merge policy must be explicit: additive-only mid-feature.** If I1 merges a schema/API the team then wants to redesign in I3, iterative delivery has ossified an early decision. Production rule: within a feature, increments merge behind the feature's integration branch with additive-only migrations; breaking changes require a roadmap-level decision entry + human ack (routes through item 14 critical). This is standard release-train hygiene; the note's "shippable increment" test needs it to be true in practice.
- **P2c. Cross-increment STALE-SPEC needs a path, not just within-increment.** Item 9 covers within-increment propagation; the §2.2 example (I1 discovery rewrites I2's outline) is *cross*-increment. The chat/finisher role's "refine roadmap" step needs the same scoped-refinement skill (planner prompt variant) + `supersedes:` linking across files. Small addition, closes the loop the worked example opens.

### 4.3 D3 — Planner does the heavy lifting; coder executes: ENDORSE+ (load-bearing — read carefully)

This is the decision the most evidence supports (Planner Matters compute-allocation; Plan-and-Act planner>executor; VeriMAP scoped executors; MAST disobey-spec 11–15% rooted in pre-execution design) and simultaneously the decision that concentrates the most risk: **the planner becomes the single point of failure, the cost center, and — after item 1 — the author of both specification and acceptance.** Three consequences:

1. **Planner quality gates must be the strictest in the system.** Item 5 (pre-check) is necessary but only checks *form* (coverage, acyclicity, executability). Nothing checks planner *judgment* except the downstream gates, which test the coder against the planner's own tests (circular — see §2.6/correction 4 and G6). Minimum viable planner-governance: (a) grounding requirements (every prescriptive STEP cites a repo path/symbol or NEW; every TESTS file cites the contracts it covers — item 5 already); (b) **eval-held-out tests** (G6) so planner judgment faces at least one planner-independent check per slice; (c) RISK-floor rules (G9) so security/data-path slices always get human eyes regardless of planner optimism.
2. **"Coder may adapt a trivial detail" needs a bright line.** "Anchored by the contracts; major drift is a REPLAN" is the right principle but not an enforceable rule — the coder is an LLM and the harness can't see intent. Operationalize: *trivial = the diff touches only files/paths listed in the slice's plan section AND all CT# verdicts stay PASS AND no new file outside the listed set.* Anything else (new file, touched file outside the section, contract edit) is auto-REPLAN (harness-detectable, no LLM judgment). This turns "major drift" from a vibe into a predicate.
3. **Coder capability floor still matters.** "Untrained executor + excellent plan" (+34pp) is a web-navigation result with a trained-planner/untrained-executor split; in repo coding, executors need tool-use fluency (edit discipline, test running, error reading) that "untrained" doesn't have. Don't read F4 as "coder models don't matter" — read it as "marginal budget goes to the planner first." Item 18's tiering rule is consistent with this; keep the coder at frontier for `outcome` slices (unknown path = executor judgment load is high).

### 4.4 D4 — Two files; slice brief as a view: ENDORSE+ (with a substrate change)

Single-source-of-truth + views is correct (artifact drift is a real MAST-adjacent failure class). **But markdown-regex as the storage substrate will not survive production — see G1.** The "two files" UX can stay exactly as the note draws it; the *source of truth* should be validated structured records (JSONL/SQLite with a schema), with the roadmap `.md` and increment-plan `.md` rendered as projections. Rationale: (a) regex extraction from LLM-emitted markdown fails on exactly the inputs LLMs produce (nested fences, reformatted headings, unicode dashes, "helpful" restructuring); (b) item 5's assertions, item 9's scoped rewrites, item 16's hash chain, and item 17's telemetry all need canonical field access — parsing markdown five different ways in five components is five drift bugs; (c) human auditability is preserved (rendered markdown is *more* consistent, not less). This is the highest-leverage engineering change in this review and it touches no evidence-backed principle — only the file format.

### 4.5 D5 — Naming (SLICE#/STEP# + CT#/GAP#/T#): ENDORSE+

Unambiguous machine-parseable anchors are validated interface design (SWE-agent −7.7pp without structured edits; MAST step-repetition as the cost of ambiguous identity; VeriMAP named I/O). Two refinements:
- **P5a. Stable IDs, not positional numbers.** `SLICE2` breaks when a slice is inserted/deleted or when item 9 rewrites a section (every downstream `DEPS:`, ledger ref, and ASK# link rots). Use stable keys (`S1a2f`-style or `I1-S2` frozen at creation + never renumbered) with the ordinal as display-only. The note's ledger already writes `I1-S2`; freeze that as the key forever.
- **P5b. One migration, as the note says — plus a compat shim.** The `S#`→`SLICE#` rename ripples through skills, extractor, and old plans. Ship the extractor with a deprecated-pattern warning (parse old, warn, don't fail) for one quarter, then fail. Prevents silent breakage of in-flight features.

### 4.6 D6 — Run shape (planner once → code-owned loop → PR → chat ships): ENDORSE+

Deterministic orchestration with LLM roles at bounded stations is the converged pattern (Phoenix, Agentless, HoH runtime, Magentic-One ledgers) and the Dissecting census favors minimal-roles-plus-scaffolding on medians. The note's upgrades list (stall states, pre-check, DEPS+worktrees, ladder, graduated autonomy) is the right backlog. Production additions:

- **P6a. Plan-vs-tree freshness check at slice start (G3).** Planner-once assumes the tree at coder time matches the tree at plan time. In any repo with concurrent human commits (i.e., production), check: `git rev-parse HEAD` + touched-paths hash vs plan-time record; on mismatch, run a cheap scoped re-ground (re-read touched files into the brief) or flag STALE-SPEC. Without this, the first concurrent commit creates heisen-slices.
- **P6b. Durable execution for the controller itself (G4).** The note durably stores artifacts but doesn't discuss controller crash recovery. Production rule: controller state is event-sourced (item 16's append-only record *is* the state — resume = replay); every side effect (commit, merge, test run, human ping) is idempotent or keyed. A harness crash mid-increment must resume, never double-apply.
- **P6c. PR composition from the tracker needs a semantic-conflict step.** Sequential slice VERIFIEDs don't imply the composition is correct (SpecBench's core lesson: features pass in isolation, fail in composition). The increment-end objective pass (item 8) + full regression re-run is the minimum; for increments with parallel-merged slices, add a semantic-merge review (human for elevated+, eval-L3 for routine). "All CT# VERIFIED → compose PR" in code is fine only if the objective pass is also a gate, not a notification.
- **P6d. Chat-role finisher needs a checklist, not a vibe.** "Ships I1, marks done, picks I2" should be: (1) objective pass green, (2) regression set green, (3) ledger distilled (item 15-lite per increment, full ritual per feature), (4) roadmap + supersedes: updated, (5) ASK# backlog triaged. A finisher that skips (3)–(5) silently rots the durable layer.

## 5. The 18 backlog items — production review

Verdict key: **SHIP** (adopt as specified, notes are operationalization) · **SHIP+** (adopt with the listed changes — changes are normative) · **STAGE** (experiment behind telemetry before committing).

### Tier 1

**Item 1 — Planner-authored tests + non-vacuity proofs. SHIP+ (highest value, highest risk — the changes are load-bearing).**
The note is right that this is the single highest-leverage upgrade (SWE-Gate 34.3% false-accept; VeriMAP mechanism; AgentCoder independence; SDD executability). It is also where the design can fail most expensively, because every downstream gate trusts these tests. Ship with five changes:
- **(a) Held-out eval tests (G6, normative).** Planner tests are *visible validation*; the eval (or a separate test-author role on the eval's model family) authors a small *held-out* suite per slice from INTENT+CT# that the coder never sees and that runs only at the gate. This is the SpecBench val/test split applied to the harness, and it is what restores authorship/acceptance separation at the planner level (correction 4). Size it small (20–30% of planner-test count, focused on CONSTRAINT-class + composition edges) to bound cost.
- **(b) Coder cannot write tests (normative).** Mount `TESTS:` paths read-only in coder worktrees; verify runs in a stage without coder write access to test paths (ImpossibleBench mitigation). Also forbid the coder from modifying `conftest.py`, test config, and CI workflow files unless the slice plan explicitly lists them (the production-RL attack list: conftest overrides, sys.exit(0), assertion monkey-patching).
- **(c) TEST-DEFECT route (G6b, normative).** When the coder or eval has evidence the *test* is wrong (contradictory assertions, impossible fixture, spec/test conflict), that verdict routes to the planner with its own budget — it must not burn repair attempts. Defect-driven gaming research shows wrong tests are where hacking concentrates; the harness must make "the test is wrong" sayable.
- **(d) Fail-before needs a mutation spot-check, not just fail-before.** Fail-before/pass-after proves the test responds to *this* change, not that it asserts the *contract*. For CONSTRAINT-class tests (the 34.3% class), require the planner to also state the test's *rejection rationale* (what wrong implementation it catches — one line per test), reviewed at L3 for elevated+ slices. Full mutation testing is overkill per slice; rationale + held-out tests + periodic hack-checks (item 17) is the affordable stack.
- **(e) Baseline snapshots must be hermetic and cheap.** "Run the repo's suite before the coder starts" per slice is O(slices × suite-time) — prohibitive on big repos. Production form: baseline once per increment (plus on tree-change, P6a), cache by tree hash, and scope per-slice baselining to the slice's touched paths + TESTS (affected-tests selection). Phoenix's baseline-awareness stands; per-slice full-suite runs don't scale.
- Contract classes (FUNCTIONAL/CONSTRAINT/PRESERVATION): keep exactly as specified — the SWE-Gate constraint taxonomy (compat, error-shape, conventions, no-new-deps, API shape) should be the initial CONSTRAINT subclass checklist in the planner skill.

**Item 2 — Stall counter + reflection + forced reset. SHIP+.]**
Magentic-One mechanics are the right reference; controller-state (not model self-report) as trigger is the right call. Changes:
- **(a) Reflection output must be ledger-typed, prose must not enter the plan (T1 resolution, normative).** REFLECT emits candidate ledger entries; FACT requires a verification ref, everything else lands as GUESS/FAILED-hypothesis. Raw reflection prose is archived to the run record (item 16) for audit, never replayed into prompts. This keeps Reflexion's benefit (verbalized lessons) while honoring F3 (no error-laden history in context).
- **(b) signature_hash needs a canonicalization spec.** Normalize: strip timestamps/PIDs/durations/absolute paths/ordering (sort lines), hash the remainder; add an embedding-similarity fallback (≥0.92 = identical) for reworded-but-same failures. Without this, "identical failure twice" never fires and the skip-to-N-version optimization is dead code.
- **(c) Flake classification needs quarantine, not just budget-burn.** Pass-on-rerun → classify flake, burn budget (as specified), *and* quarantine the test (record in ledger, exclude from pass^k streaks until it passes clean 3×, flag to planner if it's a planner-authored test — planner wrote a flaky test is a planner defect).
- **(d) Thresholds (repair≤2, stall<3) are priors — expose as config with telemetry-driven defaults per slice-type** (item 17 tunes). And keep the Magentic-One ≤2 alignment explicit in config comments so future editors know the number is evidence-pinned, not arbitrary.

**Item 3 — STEPS: prescriptive|outcome dial. SHIP.**
The predictability rule (§5.7) is the honest synthesis of a genuinely split literature (plan-first wins on predictable; constrain-outcomes wins on uncertain). Operational notes: (1) the mode tag must be *planner-declared but harness-audited* — if a `prescriptive` slice's coder deviates on >50% of steps twice, auto-flip to `outcome` on replan (the note suggests this at breach — make it automatic, it's syntactic); (2) `(auto)` mechanical steps default-to-harness (P1c); (3) log mode-per-slice-type outcomes from day one — this is the cheapest telemetry in item 17 and the input to item 18.

**Item 4 — EVIDENCE ledger with provenance grammar. SHIP+.]**
HoH's ablation (−6.28pp) makes this load-bearing, and the Magentic-One four-type grammar + supersedes-as-field is the right design. Production changes:
- **(a) Bounded reads (normative).** Planner reads ledger at increment start *through retrieval* past a cap (e.g., full read ≤8K tokens, else top-k by embedding + all PRESERVE + all open GUESS/GAP). Context-rot evidence (§3.2) makes unbounded ledger reads a reliability bug.
- **(b) Compaction/GC policy (G8, normative).** FACT→ADR promotion at feature close (durable layer absorbs it); FAILED-hypothesis entries expire after N increments unless referenced; GUESS entries must resolve (ASK# answered → decided:/assumed:, or re-verified) within the feature or escalate. A ledger without GC is a memory-interference engine (the note's own §4F risk).
- **(c) Write-permissions.** Eval appends per slice (as specified); coder proposes, never writes (coder-written "facts" about its own unverified work are self-grading); planner writes at plan/refine time. Enforce in code, not prompts.
- **(d) GUESS→ASK# auto-seed needs ranking.** Auto-promote only GUESSes that contracts depend on (as specified) AND cap blocking ASKs per increment (e.g., ≤5; overflow becomes assumed:-with-flag). Prevents human-spam from an anxious planner.

**Item 5 — Plan pre-check in code before coder tokens. SHIP+.]**
The cheapest item per unit of risk removed; extend the extractor to assertions exactly as specified. Add three checks the note omits: (1) **verify-command sandbox policy** — planner-authored commands must parse against an allowlist (test runner + paths under repo; no `curl|sh`, no `--no-verify`-style flags, no writes outside worktree) or the pre-check fails (G5 — the verify block is remote-code-execution-as-a-service until sandboxed); (2) **TESTS paths are repo-contained and non-colliding** (two slices claiming the same test file = merge collision); (3) **DEPS acyclicity + declared-paths disjointness for parallel candidates** (feeds G7). Also: pre-check failures should return *all* violations at once (planner fixes in one pass — expensive role, minimize round trips), and the check list itself should be versioned (item 16 records which pre-check version passed the plan).

**Item 6 — Distilled failure packets + brief layout contract. SHIP.**
Best-evidenced Tier-1 item (self-conditioning, PlanBench-XL explicit-signals, SWE-agent ablations, Lost-in-Middle). Notes: (1) treat the 100-line/last-5 numbers as priors from the GPT-4 era — re-ablate per model generation under item 17, keep the *mechanisms* (windowing, collapsing, guard-railed edits) fixed; (2) the failure-packet schema should add `tree_hash` + `test_version` (which TESTS revision failed — else STALE-SPEC rewrites make old packets uninterpretable); (3) guard-railed/structured edits (−7.7pp evidence) means the coder's edit tool should be a harness-provided apply-patch with dry-run + path allowlist, not freeform shell heredocs — state this explicitly, it's the difference between the evidence applying or not.

**Item 7 — Pinned invariants + re-injection. SHIP.**
Cheapest insurance; do exactly as specified. Two notes: (1) measure preamble size (cap ~1.5K tokens; overflow goes to retrieval, same rule as ledger); (2) the pinned set should include the *current slice's* CONSTRAINT-class contracts verbatim (they're the acceptance surface most likely to be "obeyed while visible, dropped after compaction").

### Tier 2

**Item 8 — Three-level eval, different family, blind, machine-readable. SHIP+.]**
Direction correct (MAST +15.6pp objective level — pending recheck; self-preference bias; SWE-Gate separation). Production changes:
- **(a) Calibrate the judge (G10, normative).** Maintain a human-labeled verdict calibration set (target: ≥100 slice-verdicts, refreshed quarterly); track eval precision/recall on REPAIR and REPLAN before trusting pass^k. An uncalibrated judge with k=2 is *consistently wrong*, not reliable. Re-calibrate on any eval-model change.
- **(b) Price it (§3.8, normative).** Eval-per-slice on a frontier different-family model + k=2 + objective pass + regression re-run is the dominant cost line. Require the §8 cost model before enabling k=2 universally; default to k=2 for elevated/critical + first-attempt slices, k=1 + sampling audit for routine (item 12 scoping).
- **(c) L2 verdicts must cite test IDs, not hunks alone** ("CT3 PASS via tests/test_login_endpoint.py::test_identical_401") — hunk citations rot on rebase; test IDs are stable and re-runnable.
- **(d) Blindness = authorship-blind + style-tolerant.** Don't include coder model/prompt in eval context (as specified); accept residual style signal (paraphrase-normalization is over-engineering at this stage — different-family is the practical control, and the calibration set in (a) measures what's left).
- **(e) Increment-end regression needs affected-test selection at scale** (full re-run of all prior verifies is O(n²); fine for 4–7 slices, needs selection beyond).

**Item 9 — STALE-SPEC propagation + supersedes:. SHIP+.]**
TDP Self-Revision is the right mechanism at the right price. The gap is operationalization: "harness flags STALE-SPEC (detect contradiction…)" is doing heavy lifting. Ship with **syntactic triggers first** (normative): downstream slice auto-flags when (i) an upstream-applied diff touches a path/symbol the downstream plan section references; (ii) a new ledger FACT references an entity the downstream TESTS/contract names; (iii) the tree hash changed under a referenced path (P6a). Semantic contradiction detection (LLM judges "does E7 invalidate SLICE4's INTENT?") is a STAGE-tier experiment — it reintroduces model judgment into the controller and must prove precision/recall before it can auto-flag (log-only mode first). Cross-increment propagation via the finisher checklist (P2c) closes the worked example.

**Item 10 — Escalation ladder + N-version. SHIP+.]**
The ladder shape is right (distilled retry → diverse search → reflect/replan → human). Changes:
- **(a) N-version selection must not be verify-only (Goodhart guard, normative).** Selecting k candidates by planner-test pass rate selects for reward hacks at k× the base rate. Selection rule: verify-pass is necessary, then eval-L2/L3 judges the *passing* candidates (or a random passing pair if >2 pass), then merge winner. Archive all candidates (as specified) — the archive is also the hack-rate sensor (if N-version winners later fail held-out tests disproportionately, the planner tests are the problem).
- **(b) Diversity via models+frames, not temperature alone.** Same-model/different-temperature candidates are correlated; prefer different-model or substantially different-framing candidates where budget allows (More Agents gains scale with effective diversity; temperature is the weakest diversifier).
- **(c) identical-failure-twice skip needs the canonicalization from item 2(b)** or it never triggers — shared dependency, build once.

**Item 11 — Slice DAG + worktree parallelism. SHIP+.]**
Industry-standard mechanism (Anthropic docs, Cursor 8-agent, Augment/MindStudio guides all converge on worktree + gates + sequential merge). The note's caution (code-isolated, runtime-not) is correct and the "start with two" rollout is correct. Production checklist additions (all normative): (1) **syntactic independence gate (G7)**: parallel launch requires disjoint write-paths (from plan sections + TESTS paths) AND no shared-migration/registry files AND DEPS satisfied — DEPS alone is planner-asserted, verify it; (2) **3-layer isolation**: worktree + per-worktree DB/schema + per-worktree ports/tmpdirs (the guides' standard stack); (3) **serialize git operations** across worktrees (shared refs corruption risk); (4) **merge in dependency order with per-merge verify + objective pass**, human review on semantic-conflict risk (tests don't cover interactions); (5) **practical ceiling 2–4 parallel** per reviewer (coordination cost dominates beyond); (6) disk/build-artifact budgets per worktree with GC on slice close.

**Item 12 — pass^k for stochastic gates. SHIP+.]**
Right metric, right scoping (k=1 deterministic). Changes: (1) **resettability requirement (§3.9, normative)**: any suite counted toward pass^k must run from deterministic fixtures (seeded RNG, frozen clock, isolated state) — else k measures infra entropy; (2) **k=2-by-default only where priced** (item 8(b) — eval k=2 is the expensive half; test k≥2 for time/net/concurrency tests is cheap and should be universal); (3) **streak semantics**: streak resets on *any* gate input change (new diff, new TESTS version, tree change) — a stale streak is a false VERIFIED; (4) the held-out slice-regression set needs a curator (add on every escaped defect; prune on suite refactor; target: stable, fast, <15 min).

**Item 13 — ASK# clarification channel. SHIP.**
Highest ROI-per-prevented-failure in Tier 2 (FM-2.2 11.65%; Ambig-DS silent-misframing; ASPI human-channel-only rule — all incorporated correctly). Notes: (1) ASK budget per increment (cap blocking ASKs; the planner must ground-first — a 30-question plan is an unread repo); (2) blocking ASKs resolve async-pre-run (don't serialize the human into the critical path more than once per increment); (3) every answered ASK# becomes decided: + (if it changed a contract) a TESTS addition — answers that don't touch tests evaporate.

**Item 14 — Graduated autonomy / RISK dial. SHIP+.]**
The trust mechanism that makes the loop shippable. One normative change: **rule-based risk floor (G9)** — RISK tags are planner-declared and planners are optimistic. Harness-enforced floor: any slice touching auth/crypto/permissions/billing/migrations/network-policy__auto-elevated__ regardless of tag (path + contract-class rules, versioned, auditable). Planner may escalate above the floor, never below. Keep the note's two-class escalation split (spec-ambiguity→human, execution-failure→auto-repair) exactly — it is the most operationally mature sentence in Tier 2.

### Tier 3

**Item 15 — Post-feature distillation ritual. SHIP the ritual, STAGE the registry.**
Split verdict: (a)+(b)+(d) — insights→ADR, patterns→plan-templates, sizing priors — SHIP: cheap, standard-practice-adjacent (ADRs), telemetry-fed. (c) skill registry with embedding retrieval — STAGE: SkillWeaver/AWM numbers are web-agent results; code-skill retrieval quality, snippet staleness, and registry rot are unproven in this loop. Ship the registry only with: versioning + usage counting + deprecation/GC policy (unused 3 features → archive; referenced-but-failing → quarantine) and a retrieval-precision measurement (does retrieved skill actually get used in the winning plan?). Without GC, the "appreciating layer" depreciates into a snippet attic.

**Item 16 — Hash-anchored tamper-evident record. SHIP+.]**
Append-only + hashed + chained is right and cheap; "empty top rung" differentiation claim is plausible. Changes: (1) canonical serialization for hashing (ties to G1 — hash the structured record, not rendered markdown); (2) secret/PII policy (G14): diffs and prompts in the record must pass a redaction pass (secret patterns, tokens, customer data) with the redaction itself logged — else the audit trail becomes a secret store; (3) retention policy (raw transcripts short-lived, hashed events + distillations long-lived).

**Item 17 — Telemetry + attribution loop. SHIP the schema, STAGE the auto-revision.**
The note gets the sequencing right (schema now, analysis later) — elevate it to a hard rule: (1) **log the assignment mechanism** (why did this slice get N-version/outcome-mode/cheap-model?) + a propensity field, or attribution will conclude "N-version causes failure" (confounding by indication — hard slices get N-version AND fail more); (2) dial changes (items 3/10/18 defaults) require small randomized experiments (the note's "ablate your own harness" caveat → requirement); (3) HarnessFix-style auto-revision of prompts/skills/gates stays STAGE until the slice-regression set + calibration set can regression-check a revision automatically — unreviewed self-modification of the harness is how you get correlated planner/eval drift that certifies its own errors (the note names this invariant — keep it load-bearing); (4) the periodic hack-check (red-team vs constraint tests) is the highest-value analysis-half practice — SHIP it early, even before the rest of the analysis half.

**Item 18 — Model tiering, planner-tagged. STAGE (conditional — as the note says, plus a guardrail).**
The condition (telemetry first) is correct; add the guardrail that makes it safe to ever enable: **tier-down only while the cheap tier's stall-rate/N-version-rate/held-out-fail-rate stays within ε of frontier on matched slice-types** (bandit with safety constraint, auto-revert on breach). Tags are planner-declared (optimism-correlated), so the guardrail must be empirical, not tag-based. Also model expected cost honestly: cheap-coder failures cost frontier-retry latency — tiering saves money only where the cheap tier's first-pass rate is high; the telemetry in item 3's mode outcomes is the input. The "never tier the eval" rule: keep.

### Considered-not-adopted: endorsed, with one revisit trigger
- Pairwise eval: correctly rejected for single-diff-against-contracts gating. **Revisit trigger:** if N-version (item 10) selection quality becomes the bottleneck, pairwise comparison of *passing candidates* is the natural selection upgrade (comparison is easier than absolute judgment — the judge literature's most robust finding).
- "Single-agent always wins" dogma: correctly rejected with the census nuance (G4 median vs G3 max). Keep the operational rule (no role without a named failure mode).
- Learned cascade now: correctly deferred. The item-18 guardrail above is the on-ramp.

## 6. Cross-cutting production gaps (G1–G15)

These are the items a senior harness engineer would raise in the design review. Ordered by production risk, not by build order (build order is §8). Each states the risk, the fix, and the acceptance criterion.

**G1. Markdown-regex is not a storage substrate. [Risk: highest — systemic brittleness]**
The note's `plan_ids.py` extractor ("grows two small accessors") will, in production, grow into the most-patched file in the repo: LLM-emitted markdown drifts (nested fences inside INTENT text, reworded headings, unicode, "helpful" renumbering), and every drift mode is a silent mis-parse (a slice the harness can't see is a slice that never runs; a verify block mis-extracted is a wrong command executed). **Fix:** schema-first artifacts. Source of truth = validated structured records (Pydantic models → JSONL rows or SQLite; JSON Schema published), with `roadmap.md` / `RM-auth-I1.md` rendered as read-only projections for humans. LLM roles emit against the schema (structured output / function calling with validation + one repair retry); item 5's assertions run on the records. **Accept:** property-test the round trip (record → render → parse → record) plus a fuzz corpus of historically-observed planner outputs; zero silent mis-parses (every parse failure is a loud pre-check error naming the field).

**G2. Positional numbering rots every cross-reference. [Risk: high — silent link rot]**
`SLICE2`, `STEP3` are positions, but item 9 rewrites sections, item 11 merges branches, and the ledger/ASK#/DEPS all link by these names. Any insert/delete/rewrite silently re-points links. **Fix:** stable unique keys at creation (`I1-S2` frozen forever; display ordinal separate), foreign-key validation in item 5 (every DEPS/ledger-ref/ASK#-target resolves or the pre-check fails). **Accept:** insert + delete + STALE-rewrite fuzz on a fixture plan; all cross-refs resolve.

**G3. No plan-vs-tree freshness. [Risk: high — heisen-slices under concurrency]**
Planner-once assumes the tree at slice time equals the tree at plan time. **Fix:** record `tree_hash + HEAD SHA + touched-paths hash` at plan time; re-check at each slice start; on mismatch do a scoped re-ground (re-read touched files into the brief) and flag STALE-SPEC if referenced symbols moved. **Accept:** concurrent-commit chaos test (human commit lands mid-increment) → slices either proceed on still-valid grounds or flag, never silently build on stale reads.

**G4. The controller itself isn't durable. [Risk: high — crash = corrupted run]**
Artifacts are durable; controller progress (which slice, which ladder rung, streaks) is implied to live in the tracker — make it explicit and crash-safe: event-sourced controller state over item 16's append-only record (resume = replay), idempotency keys on every side effect (commit/merge/verify/human-ping). **Accept:** kill -9 the harness at five different run phases on a fixture; resume completes exactly once with no double-commit/double-merge/double-ping.

**G5. Planner-authored verify commands are unaudited remote code execution. [Risk: highest-security]**
The `verify` block is LLM-authored shell that the harness runs with repo + (presumably) network + secret access. Repo content is also prompt-injection surface into the planner (ASPI logic applies beyond ASK#). **Fix (defense in depth):** (1) sandbox every verify/coder execution (container, no network default, CPU/mem/time limits, secret-less environment — tests get stub credentials); (2) item-5 command policy (allowlisted runners + repo-contained paths; deny network, privilege escalation, writes outside worktree, test-file writes per 5.1(b)); (3) provenance log of every executed command (item 16). **Accept:** red-team prompt-injection fixture set (malicious README/issue text attempting exfil/mischief via planner→verify path) — all contained, all logged.

**G6. Planner-authors-tests breaks authorship/acceptance separation at the planner level. [Risk: highest-methodological]**
Correction 4 (§2.6): planner writes spec + acceptance; all downstream gates test the coder *against the planner*. **Fix:** eval-held-out tests (small suite per slice, coder-blind, gate-only) + TEST-DEFECT route + CONSTRAINT rationale lines (§5.1 a/c/d). **Accept:** held-out suite catches ≥1 planner-test blind spot per feature in early telemetry (if it catches zero forever, either the planner is perfect or the held-out author is correlated — investigate which).

**G7. Parallel safety must be verified, not planner-asserted. [Risk: high — merge-hell + semantic corruption]**
DEPS tags are planner claims. **Fix:** syntactic independence gate before parallel launch (disjoint write-paths incl. TESTS + migrations/registries; DEPS satisfied; tree-hash-pinned bases) + dependency-ordered merges with per-merge verify + objective pass (§5.11). **Accept:** adversarial fixture (planner declares independence for overlapping slices) → harness refuses parallel launch and runs sequentially with a ledger note.

**G8. The ledger will eat the context window it was built to save. [Risk: medium-high — slow rot]**
Unbounded append + full re-read = context-rot injection into the premium role. **Fix:** bounded reads (retrieval past cap) + GC policy (FACT→ADR promotion, FAILED expiry, GUESS resolution-or-escalate) + write-permissions (§5.4). **Accept:** ledger token size per feature stays bounded across a 5-increment fixture; planner prompt tokens don't grow increment-over-increment.

**G9. Planner-declared RISK is optimistic by construction. [Risk: high — under-governed blast radius]**
**Fix:** harness-enforced rule-based risk floor (auth/crypto/perms/billing/migrations/net-policy → ≥elevated regardless of tag), versioned rules, planner may escalate only (§5.14). **Accept:** red-team fixture (planner tags a migration slice `routine`) → harness elevates + logs the override.

**G10. The eval is uncalibrated. [Risk: high — confident wrongness at scale]**
k=2 agreement measures stability, not validity. **Fix:** human-labeled calibration set (≥100 verdicts, quarterly refresh), precision/recall tracking on REPAIR/REPLAN, re-calibration on eval-model change (§5.8a). **Accept:** eval precision on REPAIR ≥0.8 before k=2-verdicts can auto-merge routine slices; below that, eval gates recommend and humans dispose (item 14 routing).

**G11. Skill-registry rot. [Risk: medium — slow value decay]**
Covered in §5.15: versioning + usage counting + deprecation/GC + retrieval-precision measurement before the registry is load-bearing. **Accept:** ≥50% of retrieved skills actually used in winning plans; zero serving of superseded snippets (supersedes: enforced at retrieval).

**G12. Human concurrency policy. [Risk: medium — daily friction]**
Humans commit to the same repo while runs execute. **Fix:** per-increment integration branch (runs build on it; humans merge to main; rebase policy at increment close with re-verify); P6a freshness checks; a "human touched my paths" notification (not a halt — bounded repair first, human only on conflict). **Accept:** documented branch policy + fixture test (human commit to main mid-run → clean rebase + re-verify, or clean conflict escalation).

**G13. No cost/latency model. [Risk: medium — budget surprise kills adoption]**
The design is eval-heavy by principle (frontier different-family eval × k=2 × per-slice + objective + regression + N-version + planner-premium). **Fix:** publish the §8 model (€/$ per slice by tier/mode/risk, tokens-per-role histograms from item 17) and a per-feature budget with graceful degradation (degrade N-version→retry, k=2→k=1+samples, frontier→cheap *in that order*, never silently). **Accept:** pilot features come within 2× of modeled cost; degradation events are ledger-logged, never silent.

**G14. Secrets/PII flow through prompts and records. [Risk: medium-high — compliance]**
Diffs → eval prompts → second vendor's API (different-family eval = data egress to another processor); diffs + prompts → item-16 permanent record. **Fix:** redaction pass on record writes (logged); eval-context minimization (diff + cited hunks, not full files); vendor/DPA review for the eval family; customer-data repos get an eval-family constraint (same-region/same-DPA vendors). **Accept:** secret-canary fixture (fake AWS key/DB URL in diff) → redacted in record, never in eval prompt beyond the citing hunk.

**G15. Buy-vs-build position. [Risk: strategic — building a parallel universe]**
GitHub Spec Kit (111k stars, 30+ integrations) + Claude Code/Codex CLIs + hooks/MCP/CI already ship the shallow SDD stack, and the ecosystem moves monthly. **Fix:** adopt ecosystem seams (constitution≈ADR vocabulary, hooks for fast checks, MCP for tools, CI for gates, gen_ai.* telemetry) and concentrate custom build on the deep half nobody ships (contract classes + non-vacuity, stall-state controller, provenance ledger, pass^k discipline, hash-anchored records, co-evolving verifiers). Revisit yearly: any Tier-3 item the ecosystem commoditizes gets deleted, not maintained. **Accept:** a one-page seam map (what we adopted vs built vs deleted) reviewed per feature-generation.

## 7. Internal tensions (T1–T4) and their resolutions

**T1. Reflection playback vs self-conditioning.** Item 2 appends REFLECT prose to plan+ledger; F3 says the model's own error-laden history is what compounds errors. Both cite good evidence (Reflexion vs 2509.09677) — the resolution is granularity + typing: *facts* persist (ledger-typed, FACT needs a verification ref), *prose* archives (item 16, never replayed into prompts). Reflexion's own bound (§3.7) supports this: reflections are only as good as the failure signal, so untyped persistence of confabulated lessons is the failure mode to design out. **Resolved by §5.2(a).**

**T2. Planner-authors-tests vs authorship/acceptance separation.** Principle 8 demands separation "at every level"; item 1 concentrates authorship (spec + tests) in the planner. The note's defense (separation preserved "in its strongest form — the coder never writes its own acceptance") answers the wrong level: the coder is separated, the planner is not. **Resolved by G6** (eval-held-out tests make the planner face one planner-independent check per slice) + TEST-DEFECT route + calibration set. With those, the separation claim reads: *no role's output is accepted solely on artifacts that role authored* — which is the enforceable form of the principle.

**T3. "No LLM deciding" vs semantic triggers.** The controller is "code, no LLM deciding" — but STALE-SPEC "contradiction detection" and stall "loop detection" are stated semantically. An LLM judging either is an LLM deciding. **Resolved by syntactic-first triggers** (§5.9: path/symbol/tree-hash overlap; §5.2(b): canonicalized signature match + embedding threshold), with semantic detection as a log-only STAGE experiment that must prove precision/recall before it can actuate. The controller stays code; judgment stays in roles whose outputs are gated.

**T4. Transferred A-grades.** Several A-graded mechanisms were measured in web/GUI/tool environments, not repo coding. The note's caveats acknowledge transfer risk; the grades don't show it. **Resolution (mechanical, no re-research):** render environment-transferred A's as **A⁻ (transfer)** wherever the primary measurement environment differs from repo-level SWE:

| Item/evidence | Grade in note | Corrected |
|---|---|---|
| Plan-and-Act numbers (WebArena) | A | A⁻ (transfer: web-nav → repo) |
| TDP −82% (TravelPlanner/ScienceWorld/HotpotQA) | A | A⁻ (transfer: tool-ques → repo) |
| Context-folding (Deep Research + SWE) | A-implied | A⁻ (transfer: partial — SWE half transfers) |

Nothing changes except honesty — and the telemetry priority: A⁻ items get ablation priority under item 17 (they're the ones most likely to shrink in transfer).

## 8. Recommended build order + cost/latency sketch

### 8.1 Build order (revised from the note's §7 sequence)

The note's Tier-1-first order is broadly right; I reorder for dependency + risk reasons (each phase ends with a working, measurable system — no phase depends on a STAGE item):

- **Phase 0 — Substrate (1–2 weeks, no LLM changes).** G1 (schema-first artifacts + rendered markdown) + G2 (stable IDs) + item 16-core (append-only hashed record) + G5-sandbox (containerized exec, secret-less env) + item 17-schema (run log format incl. assignment-mechanism fields). *Exit:* fixture plans round-trip; commands execute sandboxed; every run logs the full schema.
- **Phase 1 — The trustworthy slice loop (2–4 weeks).** Item 5 (pre-check incl. G5 command policy + TESTS containment) + item 1-core (planner TESTS + fail-before/pass-after + affected-path baselining + read-only test mounts) + item 6 (failure packets + brief layout) + item 8-core (3-level eval prompt + machine verdicts, single-family initially) + item 2-core (stall counter + canonical signatures + forced reset; REFLECT with T1 typing) + G3 (freshness) + G4 (durable resume). *Exit:* single-slice runs end-to-end with calibrated logging; flake/quarantine/discipline working on fixtures.
- **Phase 2 — The increment (2–3 weeks).** Item 3 (mode dial) + item 4 (ledger + G8 bounds/GC + write-perms) + item 9-syntactic (triggers i–iii + scoped refinement + supersedes:) + item 8-full (different-family eval + objective pass + affected regression) + item 12 (pass^k + resettability req) + G6 (held-out eval tests + TEST-DEFECT route) + G10 (calibration set bootstrap ≥30 verdicts). *Exit:* 4–7-slice increments run unassisted; held-out suite catches ≥1 planner blind spot per pilot feature.
- **Phase 3 — Throughput + trust (2–4 weeks).** Item 11 (DEPS + worktrees + G7 gate + 3-layer isolation, ceiling 2) + item 10 (ladder + N-version with §5.10 selection rule) + item 13 (ASK# + budget) + item 14 (RISK + G9 floor) + G12 (branch policy) + G14 (redaction + eval minimization). *Exit:* pilot feature ships to main with human checkpoints firing per policy; cost within 2× of model.
- **Phase 4 — Compounding (ongoing).** Item 15-ritual + templates + priors (now); item 15-registry (STAGE); item 17-analysis (hack-checks now, attribution + randomized dial experiments next, auto-revision last); item 18 (STAGE behind guardrail); G15 seam-map review. *Exit:* second feature measurably cheaper/better than the first on the §8.3 KPI set — the "iteration 10 smarter than iteration 1" claim made falsifiable.

Deferrals vs the note: item 10's N-version moves after item 8-full (selection needs a calibrated judge); item 11 moves after G7/G12 (parallelism needs safety + branch policy); item 18 stays last (as the note says).

### 8.2 Cost/latency model sketch (per slice, order-of-magnitude — replace with pilot telemetry)

Tokens (relative units where 1u ≈ a small coder attempt): planner amortized ~2–4u/slice (premium model, amortized over 4–7 slices + TESTS authorship + held-out authorship); coder 1u × attempts (1 + repair-rate×ladder-cost; N-version ×k on the failing minority); eval 0.5–1.5u × k (frontier different-family, diff+tests+rubric) + objective-pass amortized + regression compute. **Expected dominant lines:** (1) eval tokens (k=2 doubles the single most expensive call); (2) planner premium (TESTS authorship roughly doubles planner output vs prose-only plans); (3) N-version bursts (bounded by failing-minority × k). Compute (verify/regression) is the latency line on big repos — affected-test selection (5.1e/5.8e) is what keeps it sublinear. **Budget rule:** price a feature at (slices × slice-p50 × 2.5× contingency) until item 17 replaces priors; degrade in the G13 order (N-version → retry, k=2 → k=1+samples, frontier → cheap), ledger-logged, never silent.

### 8.3 What "great" looks like (KPIs — leading + lagging)

- **Lagging (system works):** pass^k over the held-out slice-regression set (item 12, resettability-required); escaped-defect rate per shipped increment (post-merge failures incl. maintainer-revert equivalents); cost-per-shipped-slice vs model.
- **Leading (loop healthy):** stall-breach rate, N-version invocation rate, TEST-DEFECT rate (planner-test quality sensor), held-out fail rate (same), eval calibration precision/recall, ASK# blockers-per-increment + answer-latency, human-override rate on RISK floor (planner optimism sensor), ledger-size-per-feature (GC health), pre-check first-pass rate (planner discipline).
- **Meta (compounding):** second-feature vs first-feature deltas on cost, stall rate, and escaped defects — the falsifiable form of the appreciating-layer thesis.
